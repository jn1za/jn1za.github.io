## Emerging in Computer Science

ICS314 has become my most profound computer science course that I’ve taken here at UH Manoa to date. As a student in computer science, the field of study on its own feels extremely  *massive* with all sorts of paths you take yourself on with all different kinds of work you would experience in those specialties. I used to be one who felt I had to commit to a certain area from the get go, and feel assured that I had a plan mapped out for years to come. The more I learn as a growing student, a growing adult, and a person who simply wants to pursue what I enjoy the most, the only way to know that is to really try to enjoy everything. As ironic as it is to say, I wouldn’t know if I liked a speciality until I experienced it. 

Here in this Software Engineering I course, I got to dabble in this particular practice, and being able to use the software and processes used by real professionals and developers in the current times truly gives me the feeling that I am experiencing what it would be like as a true software engineer. It draws the picture of what that would look like for me in greater detail. Through this class, I was able to strengthen my awareness of computer science and programming practices, technical skills in web development, as well as grow my understanding of the world that encompasses technology. 

## Open Source Programming: The Stacking Giants

Before ICS314, I had known little of Git or GitHub, just that they were constantly mentioned and seemed to be very standard knowledge to others when having discussions in this computer science field. Ironically, I did want to be a part of knowing what that is, as it seemed pretty pivotal should I progress deeper in this field. Little did I know that I’d be so heavily involved with Git by the end of this class and produce an entire open source developed web application, live and deployed for a community’s real use, and with a team of peers as developing through GitHub. Not only do I know what it's like to develop as a team, but I see the importance in using shared resources, maintaining version control, documenting tasks in a project, and more which start to expand all that this is affecting. I am engaging in something bigger than myself, and closer to what had been initially wished. 

In fact, the very reason I had proposed the idea for the final project, which had ultimately became what [Project: TeamUHp](https://team-uhp.github.io/) was because of the same situation where I felt I could dream up many ideas, but I felt they might be out of scope from what I may have learned so far or have been capable to do at the time. TeamUHp strived to narrow that gap by imploring connections for collaboration, and in the same way, I have closed that gap myself by growing my technical abilities and engaging myself with open source programming.

In this course, I have realized better what it's like to be, not only a programmer or a ‘coder’, but a software engineer. It’s like finally being able to speak the language of the foreign country you reside in, and being an active participant in this world. This is particular for open source programming, as I realize that great creations are a composite of efforts and ideas of all the engineers who have created some foundational groundwork, and the engineers who will take them on and grow into something new. The more I use GitHub, the videos of all the computer science content creators which I watch, make a lot more sense, and the more I realize how the precise nature of open source is made to entice innovation. 

This idea of growth through the interweaving of many independent minds always reminds me of what Newton said on breakthroughs: "If I have seen further, it is by standing on the shoulders of giants.” Being in this field, this quote resonates with my understanding of this growing world. Just as the mountains grow through the piling of layers of earth, technology has grown in all these decades of continuous development and sharing of ideas, and ultimately is anticipated to carry this giant dream into the era where it is made the new reality.

## UI Frameworks & The Guiding Design

Prior to the final project, one of the first moments that I had truly felt I was being a creator of the very things that I could only consume day-to-day was in the whole section of UI Design and UI Frameworks. Every time I use an application, I do have to wonder what it takes to make that usable to the end-user, update the necessary fetched information, and reflect whatever changes for everyone else in the app. Of course, this requires a lot more implementation and reserved resources than the UI, but the first thing that any regular person would see and recognize is the visual build.

If there was one thing that I knew I wanted to know right away, it was the way that web pages are built and displayed. There are times I cannot stand having this be a mystery, because it is so obviously used by the everyday person, everyday. Somehow, the progression into these frameworks built me a sort of implicit history lesson of the internet. 

When we started with the basic HTML, all those old-school blog pages of the 2000s and prior started to make a lot of sense. Learning this inherently gave history as to why the internet looked the way it did back then. Moving ahead in time, as we grew into using Bootstrap and React components, it compliments the idea that the more modern look has been created by means of a long developed, open source resource which was ultimately shared until it became what we know to be today’s standard. Interestingly, naturally built standards are what is known to the user as their intuition for navigating an app, even if it’s something they’ve opened for the first time. This informs that the purpose of UI design is more than to present interactable functionality, but to guide the experience and composition of the application as a whole. 

## This World is My Own Too

Somehow, through simply walking through these topics as lessons in a course, it has allowed me to map them to their instances in the real world, and the implications it shows in the field of study. Not only am I a student of technology, but the entire world is a consumer of it as a way of living. More than this course is a class in my pathway, it informs all else of that which I wish to pursue, not only in computer science or in a technical job, but one who wishes to understand the world and create solutions to the collective issues the world faces. At face value, such a statement seems far fetched and too big for me. However, if there is a special lesson that this course has taught me, it’s that the overwhelming and the complex can be approached by effectively learning and diligently *doing*. Working with modern tools such as GitHub, UI frameworks, and their subsequent team-based development processes in the final project development, it has shown me what it truly means to contribute to a living system; a system that evolves through collective effort and shared responsibility; a system by which the modern times have become what it is today. Though this field of study and this world of technology is vast, this world is my own too, and I am an active contributor to it.
